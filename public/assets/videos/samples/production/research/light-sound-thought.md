# Light Vs Sound Vs Thought

Research checked September 11, 2026. Production brief for a factual cinematic Short; proposed narration is 80 words.

## Checked Claims And Necessary Qualifier

| Claim | Status And Safe Wording | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light travels at 299,792,458 m/s | Exact for light in vacuum. “Almost 300 million meters a second” is appropriate narration rounding. Do not silently apply the vacuum value to light in water or glass. | [NIST CODATA constant](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/Value/c.html) |
| Sound travels at about 343 m/s | Appropriate for air near 20 °C; the medium and conditions matter. NASA's acoustics reference gives 343 m/s for air at 20 °C. This is not sound's universal speed. | [NASA technical reference, Chapter VIII, Table 3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160000818/downloads/20160000818.pdf) |
| Nerve impulses span a wide range of speeds | Use a representative range, roughly 0.5 to over 100 m/s, rather than a universal bound. A physiology reference gives unmyelinated axons about 0.5–10 m/s and some myelinated axons up to 150 m/s. These figures concern propagation along axons, not the rate of thinking. | [NCBI-hosted physiology reference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10921/) |
| The slow end has direct human measurements | A human microneurography study reports C-fiber group means near 0.7–0.8 m/s and differences related to activity. It does not measure whole thoughts or establish a universal minimum. | [Original human C-fiber study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2269177/) |
| Thought has one speed in meters per second | Unsupported framing. Propagation velocity, processing latency, and information throughput are different quantities. A 2024/2025 perspective discusses about 10 bits/s for behavior; a 2025 response challenges extending that ceiling to overall neural processing. Neither is a speed in m/s. | [Original Neuron perspective](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.11.008), [Nature Neuroscience response](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01997-0) |

**Required qualifier:** this compares physical signal speeds under stated conditions, then explains why thought is not a third object on the same racetrack. Neural speed depends on fiber properties and conditions; thinking involves network processing and synaptic interactions. An imagined journey does not represent information traveling to the imagined location.

## Narration

> Could a thought outrun a thunderclap? Light in a vacuum travels almost 300 million meters a second. Sound in air around twenty degrees Celsius manages about 343. Nerve impulses vary enormously: roughly half a meter to over a hundred meters a second, depending on the fiber. But that isn’t the speed of thought. Thinking involves signals and processing across networks. Imagining the Moon instantly doesn’t send anything there. A signal’s travel speed and a thought’s processing time are different measurements.

Delivery: energetic opening, precise but relaxed numerical readout, then slow slightly for “that isn’t the speed of thought.” Read 343 as “three hundred forty-three.” Never change the ending to “thought is faster than light.”

## Four Cinematic Scientific Visuals

1. **Flash, then thunder:** a distant lightning flash above a mountain valley; hold a brief gap before thunder. Use this as a familiar hook, with any delayed audio marked as an illustrative timing choice rather than a measured storm distance.
2. **Three physical scales:** a graphic vacuum light path, air pressure waves at “20 °C,” and an axon traveling signal. Add all figures and units in postproduction. Use separate scaled panels rather than pretending their slowed animations share a real-time speed.
3. **Nerve close-up:** contrast a thin unmyelinated axon with a myelinated axon and its nodes. Show a moving activation highlight as an explanatory convention, not literal light or a spark running through tissue. Label “Axon propagation—not thought speed.”
4. **The imagined Moon:** an eye closes, a Moon image appears, then the view returns to a brain-network graphic. Keep the Moon image within a thought bubble or framing device; finish on “Travel speed ≠ processing time.” Do not animate a signal shooting from the brain to the Moon.

- Zheng and Meister, *The unbearable slowness of being: Why do we live at 10 bits/s?*: [authors' published perspective](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.11.008). Published online in 2024; journal issue dated 2025. It is not a measurement of thoughts moving through space.
- Sauerbrei and Pruszynski (2025), *The brain works at more than 10 bits per second*: [authors' scientific response](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01997-0).

The narration deliberately omits a single bits-per-second claim because it would distract from the distinction between propagation and cognition. No assets or audio were generated for this research brief.
