# El Niño 2026 — research and short-video script

Verified on September 11, 2026. Intended length: approximately 35–42 seconds. Treat the dates and probabilities as a September 2026 snapshot; recheck before later publication.

## Narration

This year's El Niño could make history. NOAA's September tenth forecast gives it a seventy-five percent chance this fall of beating past events in a record going back to nineteen fifty. The difference? Exceptional ocean heat, including deep below the Pacific's surface, is helping it strengthen. For America's winter, forecasts favor a wetter South and warmer North. Elsewhere, shifting rain patterns raise drought and flood risks. But stronger doesn't mean worse everywhere. El Niño changes the odds. It can't predict the weather on your street.

Voice direction: conversational, alert, slightly incredulous on the opening, then grounded. Small pause after “The difference?” and before the last two sentences. Avoid a trailer voice, robotic stress on every noun, and a sales-style closing. Pronounce Niño “NEEN-yo.”

## Claim checks and sources

- **Confirmed / NOAA, issued September 10, 2026:** An El Niño Advisory is active; the ocean and atmosphere show an event strengthening through August. **Forecast:** above 90% chance of a very strong event in Northern Hemisphere fall/winter 2026–27; 75% chance October–December 2026 exceeds previous events since 1950, defined in this forecast as a three-month RONI of at least +2.5°C. “Could make history” is conditional, not a record already achieved. [NOAA CPC September diagnostic discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_disc_sep2026/ensodisc.shtml)
- **Independent confirmation / WMO, published September 3, 2026:** The report is titled *August 2026* and describes conditions as of mid-August, with nearly 100% probability of persistence through December–February 2026–27. This is an older observation window than the NOAA release, not a September 3 measurement. [WMO August 2026 update](https://wmo.int/resources/publication-series/el-ninola-nina-updates/august-2026)
- **Why this event stands out / WMO, September 3, 2026:** Exceptional surface and subsurface Pacific warmth supports further strengthening. The ocean-atmosphere response can reorganize rainfall and temperature worldwide, with flood, drought, and heat risks varying by season and region. Event strength alone does not determine local severity; other ocean basins also matter. The script's “difference” refers to the unusual heat and forecast magnitude, not a newly invented mechanism. [WMO event briefing](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/el-nino-set-become-very-strong-raising-risks-of-extreme-weather-2027)
- **U.S. impacts / NOAA CPC, issued August 20, 2026, 8:30 a.m. EDT:** The latest seasonal discussion verified here favors a warmer northern U.S. during winter and increased precipitation chances across the South, including California and the Southeast. The wet signal strengthens into winter; fall impacts need not match winter. The September 17 replacement had not yet been issued at this research cutoff. Use this August outlook only for regional effects; its 69% record-strength probability is superseded by September's 75%. [CPC seasonal outlook discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/predictions/90day/fxus05.html)
- **Measurement context / NOAA CPC, February 2026:** CPC adopted the Relative Oceanic Niño Index, comparing Niño-region warming with the global tropics to reduce the influence of background tropical warming. Do not compare raw weekly sea-surface anomalies with three-month RONI strength categories. The precise day is not printed on the announcement page. [CPC RONI announcement](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/announcement.php)

The NOAA ENSO blog was searched, but no current September 2026 article was independently verified. Its landing page returned a JavaScript verification screen. No blog claim is used as evidence here. The dated CPC diagnostic discussion is the primary current-status source.

## Concrete visual sequence

1. **0–3 seconds / hook:** Full-bleed vertical overhead Pacific ocean shot, hard cut to a globe centered on the Pacific. Large restrained caption: “THIS EL NIÑO COULD MAKE HISTORY.” No service title or app framing.
2. **3–12 seconds / evidence:** Warm equatorial Pacific overlay over a geographically correct Earth. “75% CHANCE” appears once, followed by “OCT–DEC 2026 · NOAA, SEP 10.” Small source qualifier: “Exceeding past events since 1950 · RONI.” If the map is illustrative, label it “Illustration”; do not invent a measured temperature scale or fabricate a NOAA forecast map.
3. **12–20 seconds / mechanism:** Cinematic cut below the ocean surface. A restrained colored cross-section shows unusual heat below the surface, then arrows and cloud movement connect ocean and atmosphere. Clearly an explanatory animation, not satellite or underwater measurement footage. No boiling ocean or glowing lava.
4. **20–29 seconds / personal relevance:** Rain hitting a southern U.S. street; cut to a northern winter landscape with a modest warm-tinted probability overlay. Small captions read “WETTER SOUTH FAVORED” and “WARMER NORTH FAVORED.” Do not imply rain or absent snow is guaranteed.
5. **29–35 seconds / global consequences:** Match-cut a dry field to heavy tropical rain. Caption “RISKS SHIFT BY REGION.” Use generic illustrative footage; do not identify a real 2026 disaster without separate verification.
6. **35–42 seconds / payoff:** Return to an ordinary street under changing clouds, with the last line as subtitles. Optional tiny Geo Data mark in the final second only. Music resolves quietly instead of a dramatic disaster stinger.

Uncertainty must remain in the spoken edit: “could,” “chance,” “forecasts favor,” and the closing odds explanation. Do not replace these with “will be the strongest ever,” “everywhere will flood,” or a city-level promise. The record comparison starts in 1950 and uses RONI; it does not mean strongest in Earth's history. No specific hurricane, food-price, power-grid, or health outcome is established by these sources.
