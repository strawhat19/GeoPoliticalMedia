## Narration

This glass can survive a hammer blow. But snap its tiny tail, and the whole thing shatters. It's a Prince Rupert's drop. Molten glass hardens outside first in cold water. As the center cools and shrinks, it traps stress inside. The surface is squeezed tight. The core stays under tension. Break the tail, and cracks race into the head... tearing the entire drop apart.

## Scientific basis

- [Aben et al., Applied Physics Letters (2016), On the extraordinary strength of Prince Rupert's drops](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4971339): high surface compressive stresses explain the strength of the head. Resistance is conditional; the script says it **can** survive a blow, not that it is unbreakable.
- [Cashman, Liu and Rust, PNAS (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2202856119): rapid external cooling creates a compressive exterior and tensile interior; breaking the tail initiates rapid fragmentation.

The cutaway colors and slow-motion fracture are explanatory animation. They are not thermal measurements or physically timed fracture footage. Fragmentation is shown without fire or an explosive shockwave.
